# net/netlink/ — Netlink Message Machinery

## Key invariants
- **genl family-id is dynamic** (resolved at runtime via `CTRL_CMD_GETFAMILY`); nfnl subsystem id is a compile-time `NFNL_SUBSYS_*` constant — hence genl needs the runtime registry, nfnl's is simpler.
- **genl dump flag follows the cmd's handlers** — the same reply says which of a family's ops have a doit, a dumpit or both; `genl_family_rcv_msg()` routes on `NLM_F_DUMP` and rejects a request whose routed handler is missing, so a dumpit-only cmd is sent the dump bits and a doit-only cmd neither, while both-or-unknown cmds and the 1-in-10 pure-chaos flags words are left to the random pick. The controller's ops are fixed, so GETPOLICY, its dumpit-only cmd, is pinned without a reply.
- **rtnl dump flag follows the GET's handlers too** — `rtnetlink_rcv_msg()` routes a GET to its dumpit on either dump bit and answers `-EOPNOTSUPP` when the routed handler is missing under the request's family and under `PF_UNSPEC`. The GETs with only a dumpit under every family (`RTM_GETNEIGHTBL`, `RTM_GETRULE`, `RTM_GETMULTICAST`, `RTM_GETANYCAST`, `RTM_GETVLAN`, `RTM_GETTUNNEL`) are sent both dump bits and `RTM_GETDCB`, which has only a doit, neither (`rtnl_get_caps()` in `msg-core.c`), through the same pin as genl. A GET whose doit depends on the family (`RTM_GETADDR`, `RTM_GETROUTE`, `RTM_GETNETCONF`) and the chaos flags words are left to the random pick. `tests/rtnl_gen_test.c` holds the dump bits to that model.
- **genl requests mostly clear the genl core's header check** — for a cmd at or past the family's `resv_start_op` (every cmd when it is left 0), `genl_header_check()` rejects a nonzero `genlmsghdr.reserved` and any flag outside REQUEST/ACK/ECHO and the dump pair before it looks the op up. A genl message therefore draws `NLM_F_ATOMIC` and the NEW/DELETE modifiers one time in 8 rather than every time (older families skip the check, and handlers such as gtp's read them), and carries a nonzero reserved word only when it is a chaos message. Other protocols draw the modifiers every time.
- **An nfnl batch has to open the buffer** — `nfnetlink_rcv()` sends a whole skb down the batch path only when its first message is `NFNL_MSG_BATCH_BEGIN`, and a lone `NFNL_CB_BATCH` command is answered `-EINVAL`. `netlink_gen_msg()` picks the first message's type itself and, when that names a batch callback (`nfnl_type_is_batch()`), opens a batch seven times in eight: BEGIN, batch callbacks of the same subsystem and END, which is left off one batch in eight, and `nfnetlink_rcv_batch()` then aborts with `NFNL_ABORT_VALIDATE` unless a command failed. The commands of a batch mostly share one `nfgen_family`, picked from the subsystem's grammar, because nf_tables finds a table by family and name together; a lone message mostly names one of its subsystem's families as well. A batch callback that arrives later in a multi-message buffer is left bare.
- **The multicast and anycast requests are address dumps** — `RTM_GETMULTICAST` has a dumpit for `PF_INET`, `PF_INET6` and, since `dev_mc_dump()` (`net/core/dev_addr_lists.c`), `PF_PACKET`, and `RTM_GETANYCAST` one for `PF_INET6` alone; neither has a doit, so both are sent the dump bits (above). `gen_rtnl_body_addr_dump()` builds the `ifaddrmsg` they read, with a family the type has a dumpit for most of the time and `ifa_prefixlen`, `ifa_flags` and `ifa_scope` 0 seven times in eight, as `dev_mc_valid_dump_req()` demands of the `PF_PACKET` dump and the IPv4 and IPv6 dumps do of a socket that asks for strict checking. `IFA_TARGET_NETNSID`, an `NLA_S32`, is the one attribute they parse, and a strict parse refuses any other. `RTM_GETADDR`'s header (`gen_rtnl_body_addr()`) keeps to the same, prefixlen, flags and scope 0 seven times in eight and the index 0, lo or a small one, since the IPv4 and IPv6 address dumps and the IPv6 doit hold a strict socket's GET to it; `RTM_NEWADDR` and `RTM_DELADDR` draw all of them at random. `tests/rtnl_gen_test.c` holds the requests to a model of that check.
- **The link size limits are drawn inside what the kernel takes** — `ifla_policy` holds `IFLA_GSO_MAX_SIZE` and `IFLA_GSO_IPV4_MAX_SIZE` to at least `MAX_TCP_HEADER + 1`, 513 at most, and `validate_linkmsg()` refuses either above the device's `tso_max_size`, 65536 until a driver raises it (lo and veth do, to the BIG TCP maximum of `8 * 65535`), and `IFLA_GRO_MAX_SIZE` and `IFLA_GRO_IPV4_MAX_SIZE` above that maximum. `gen_rta_link_payload()` therefore draws the GSO limits from 1024 up, mostly to 65536, a quarter of them within a few segments and one in eight a BIG TCP size, and the GRO limits from 0 to the maximum; a limit under the size of the skbs a flow sends is what makes a device re-segment them. `IFLA_PACING_OFFLOAD` is 0 or 1, and a device with no pacing horizon, which is every virtual one, answers a 1 `-EOPNOTSUPP`. `tests/rtnl_gen_test.c` holds the draws to that model.
- **The neighbour table's probe interval is drawn inside its range** — `nl_ntbl_parm_policy` holds `NDTPA_INTERVAL_PROBE_TIME_MS` to `nl_ntbl_parm_ms_range`, 1000 to 86400000 ms, and a random u64 is inside it about once in 2^38, so `build_ndta_parms_nested()` (`msg-rtnl-neigh.c`) draws it there seven times in eight, half of those within ten seconds, where `neigh_managed_work()` reruns often, and leaves the eighth as random bytes. The other `NDTPA_*` timers take any u64 and stay random. `tests/rtnl_gen_test.c` holds the draws to that range.
